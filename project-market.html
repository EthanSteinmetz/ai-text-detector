Objective

Why a Macro Dashboard?

Before positioning in equities, a strategist or S&T professional needs to understand the macro environment. Interest rates, credit conditions, volatility, and sector momentum all feed into the risk-on / risk-off framework that governs short-term market behavior and medium-term asset allocation strategy.

This project involved identifying the most relevant macro indicators, sourcing the data, building a Python-based dashboard using Plotly/Dash for visualization, and interpreting the signals to generate a sector rotation view and overall market bias recommendation.

Dashboard Signal (Q4 2024): The 2Y–10Y yield curve has uninverted (+18 bps), VIX has normalized below 20, and IG credit spreads are tight at 85 bps — collectively pointing to a Risk-On environment. Recommended positioning: Overweight Financials, Technology, and Industrials; Underweight Utilities and Long-Duration Bonds.

Tools Used

Python (Dash, Plotly) pandas / NumPy FRED API (Federal Reserve) Yahoo Finance API Microsoft Excel Bloomberg Academic

Key Finance Concepts

  • Yield curve shape — normal, inverted, uninverting dynamics
  • Credit spreads — IG vs HY spread as risk appetite indicator
  • VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) — implied volatility and fear gauge
  • Fed Funds Rate and its effect on equity valuations
  • Sector rotation — cyclical vs defensive asset positioning
  • Risk-on / risk-off framework for asset allocation
  • Economic cycle indicators — ISM, PMI, jobless claims

Methodology

Dashboard Build Process

01

Select the Indicator Framework

Identified 12 key macro variables organized into 4 categories: Rates (Treasury yields, Fed Funds, TIPS breakevens), Credit (IG spreads, HY spreads, CDX), Equity (VIX, AAII sentiment, put/call ratio), and Growth (ISM Manufacturing, Jobless Claims, PMI).

02

Build the Data Pipeline

Connected to the FRED API for Treasury yields and credit data; Yahoo Finance API for equity market data and sector ETF prices. Automated the data pull into a pandas DataFrame, updating daily.

03

Design the Signal Framework

Built a rule-based signal engine: each indicator is scored +1 (bullish), 0 (neutral), or -1 (bearish) based on predefined thresholds. Aggregate score determines the overall Risk-On / Neutral / Risk-Off bias.

04

Build the Plotly Dashboard

Created a multi-panel Plotly Dash application with time-series charts for each indicator category, a sector heatmap, and a summary scorecard. Color-coded signaling (green/yellow/red) for intuitive readability.

05

Derive Sector Rotation Recommendations

Mapped the aggregate signal to a sector rotation framework: at what stage of the economic cycle are we, and what sectors historically outperform at that stage? Generated a formal market strategy note summarizing the view.

What I Learned

Key Takeaways

Yield Curve as a Leading Indicator

The 2Y–10Y spread uninverting historically precedes equity rallies — but also sometimes signals a recession has already arrived. Context matters enormously.

Credit Spreads Anticipate Equities

IG and HY spreads often tighten before equities rally. Building a cross-asset view rather than watching equities in isolation was a major learning.

Data Pipeline Skills

Connecting to APIs (FRED, Yahoo Finance), cleaning data, and building a reproducible pipeline is as important as the analysis itself.

Rules vs. Judgment

Rule-based signals provide discipline but can lag during inflection points. Good macro analysis blends quantitative signals with qualitative judgment.

Why It Matters for Finance

Essential for S&T and Macro Strategy

Sales and Trading professionals — especially on rates, credit, and equity desks — need to contextualize every trade within the macro environment. A buy or sell decision in isolation misses the broader picture; understanding where yields are headed, how tight credit spreads are, and what the yield curve shape implies for bank earnings fundamentally shapes positioning.

Macro strategy roles at hedge funds, banks (global markets strategy), and asset managers explicitly require this kind of cross-asset thinking and the ability to synthesize diverse data into a coherent market view.

Building this dashboard also taught me the data engineering skills increasingly required for quantitative roles: API integration, data pipeline construction, and interactive visualization.
