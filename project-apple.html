Objective

Investment Thesis

This project replicates the format and analytical rigor of a buy-side equity research note. The goal was to form an independent investment view on Apple by synthesizing qualitative business analysis, financial modeling, and relative valuation — ultimately arriving at a price target and investment rating.

Thesis Summary: Apple is a BUY at current prices. The Services segment ($96B run-rate, 28%+ gross margin) is systematically undervalued relative to pure-play software peers. Combined with accelerating AI-enabled device refresh cycles and $110B+ in annual capital returns, Apple offers a compelling risk-adjusted return profile. Base case price target: $198.

Tools Used

Microsoft Excel FactSet (Academic) SEC EDGAR (10-K) Bloomberg Academic DCF Model Comparable Company Analysis

Key Finance Concepts

  • Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) — intrinsic value derivation
  • EV/EBITDA and P/E — relative valuation multiples
  • Bull / Base / Bear scenario analysis
  • Gross margin expansion and Services mix shift
  • Free Cash Flow (FCF) yield and capital return programs
  • Terminal growth rate and WACC sensitivity
  • Revenue segmentation and unit economics

Methodology

Process & Approach

01

Business & Industry Overview

Mapped Apple's revenue segments (iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables, Services), assessed competitive positioning, and identified the key fundamental drivers: Services monetization, emerging markets penetration, and AI hardware integration.

02

Financial Statement Analysis

Pulled 5 years of income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements from the 10-K. Calculated historical growth rates, margin profiles, FCF conversion, and balance sheet efficiency metrics.

03

Build the DCF Model

Projected 5 years of free cash flow under three scenarios (bull, base, bear). Applied a WACC of 8.8% derived from CAPM with beta of 1.25 and current risk-free rate. Applied a 3% terminal growth rate in base case.

04

Comparable Company Analysis (Comps)

Selected a peer group of 6 companies: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Samsung, Meta, and Qualcomm. Screened on EV/EBITDA, P/E (NTM), and FCF yield. Derived an implied peer-based valuation range.

05

Synthesize Price Target & Rating

Weighted DCF (60%) and comps (40%) to derive a blended price target of $198 in the base case. Established a formal BUY rating with a 12-month investment horizon.

What I Learned

Key Takeaways

DCF Sensitivity

Small changes in WACC (±50 bps) or terminal growth (±50 bps) move the price target by $15–$25. This taught me to never anchor on a single DCF output.

Segment Mix Matters

Apple's gross margin expansion story is almost entirely driven by Services growth (74% gross margin vs 37% for Products). This has huge implications for valuation.

Comps Selection

Choosing the right peer group is subjective but critical. Apple is part hardware, part software platform — no single comp captures the full picture.

Structuring a Pitch

A good stock pitch has a clear, concise thesis, not just numbers. Learning to translate model outputs into a compelling narrative was the hardest part.

Why It Matters for Finance

Core Skill for Equity Research & AM

A stock pitch is the foundational deliverable in buy-side portfolio management, equity research, and many S&T interview processes. The ability to form a clear investment view and defend it with rigorous analysis is what separates analysts from order-takers.

This project required synthesizing accounting knowledge (reading financials), corporate finance (DCF), relative valuation (comps), and qualitative judgment (business thesis) — the exact skill stack for roles at asset managers, hedge funds, and equity research desks.

Practicing on a well-known company like Apple forced discipline: with so much public information available, the work needs to go beyond surface-level facts to a genuine insight about value.
