Objective

Building a DCF From First Principles

A discounted cash flow model is the foundational valuation methodology in investment banking, equity research, and private equity. The objective of this project was to build a complete DCF for Microsoft — from scratch, using only publicly available data from the 10-K — to arrive at an intrinsic value estimate and assess whether MSFT is fairly valued.

Microsoft was selected because it is a complex, multi-segment business (Productivity & Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, More Personal Computing) that requires careful revenue segmentation, margin analysis, and growth rate assumptions — making it an ideal subject for a rigorous modeling exercise.

Model Conclusion: At a base-case WACC of 8.5% and terminal growth rate of 2.5%, Microsoft's intrinsic value is approximately $387 per share — roughly 9% below the prevailing market price of ~$425. The model suggests MSFT is moderately overvalued at current levels, though the bull case (WACC 7.8%, TGR 3.0%) supports a $448 fair value. Conclusion: HOLD / Accumulate on weakness.

Tools Used

Microsoft Excel SEC EDGAR (10-K) MSFT FY2024 Annual Report CAPM (WACC Derivation) Damodaran Beta Database US Treasury Yield Curve

Key Finance Concepts

  • Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) — unlevered cash flow projection
  • Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) — via CAPM and debt cost
  • Terminal Value — Gordon Growth Model (perpetuity growth)
  • Enterprise Value — sum of PV of FCFs + PV of terminal value
  • Equity Bridge — EV minus net debt to arrive at equity value
  • Sensitivity analysis — impact of ±50bps on WACC and TGR
  • CapEx and D&A treatment — free cash flow normalization

Methodology

Model Build Process

01

Historical Financial Statement Analysis

Pulled 5 years of income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements from MSFT's 10-K filings. Calculated historical revenue growth rates by segment, EBIT margins, CapEx intensity, D&A as % of revenue, and working capital dynamics.

02

Revenue and Margin Projections (Years 1–5)

Projected revenue by segment (Intelligent Cloud driven by Azure at 20%+ CAGR; Productivity stable at 10–12%; MPC declining modestly). Built bottom-up EBIT margin forecasts reflecting operating leverage and continued R&D investment in AI infrastructure.

03

Free Cash Flow Build

Calculated FCFF = EBIT × (1 – Tax Rate) + D&A – CapEx – Change in Net Working Capital. Applied a normalized 20% effective tax rate and projected CapEx in line with Azure expansion guidance.

04

WACC Derivation

Cost of equity via CAPM: Risk-free rate 4.42% (10Y Treasury) + Beta 0.92 × Equity Risk Premium 5.5% = 9.47% cost of equity. Cost of debt: 3.8% pre-tax (per 10-K), 3.04% after-tax. Debt/total capital: 12%. Blended WACC: 8.5%.

05

Terminal Value & Equity Bridge

Applied Gordon Growth terminal value using Year 5 FCF × (1 + g) / (WACC – g) where g = 2.5%. Discounted terminal value back to present. Summed PV of FCFs + PV of TV = Enterprise Value. Subtracted net debt ($40.8B) and divided by diluted share count (7.44B).

06

Sensitivity Analysis

Built a 5×5 sensitivity table varying WACC (7.5% to 9.5%) and terminal growth rate (1.5% to 3.5%) to illustrate how assumption changes drive valuation — the most important lesson of any DCF exercise.

What I Learned

Key Takeaways

WACC Construction

WACC isn't just a number you look up — understanding its components (risk-free rate, beta, ERP, debt cost, capital structure) is essential for defending your valuation.

Terminal Value Dominates

79% of MSFT's enterprise value comes from terminal value. This taught me to spend as much time on the terminal assumptions as the near-term projections.

Garbage In, Garbage Out

The model is only as good as the assumptions feeding it. Anchoring to the business fundamentals — not the price — is the only way to build a credible DCF.

Reading the 10-K

Understanding how MSFT reports revenue by segment, treats stock-based compensation, and discloses capital allocation priorities is half the modeling work.

Why It Matters for Finance

Foundation of IB, Equity Research & PE

DCF modeling is the cornerstone of intrinsic valuation across investment banking (M&A fairness opinions, LBO modeling), equity research (price target setting), and private equity (deal underwriting). No serious analyst in any of these roles can avoid becoming proficient in DCF construction and analysis.

Beyond the mechanics, the DCF forces disciplined thinking about a business: its growth trajectory, competitive moat, reinvestment needs, and capital cost. Building one from scratch — rather than inheriting a template — exposes every assumption and forces you to defend each number.

The sensitivity analysis framework is directly applicable to risk management thinking on a trading desk: what moves the needle, and by how much?
