Objective

Applying Finance to a Real Sales Context

This project was designed to demonstrate how core capital budgeting concepts — IRR, NPV, and payback period — apply directly in a sales and advisory context. Solar energy sales is an industry where the financial justification for installation is the central selling proposition: customers buy based on economics, not just environmental preference.

I built a flexible Excel model (with a Python version for scenario iteration) that takes system size, installation cost, local utility rates, and available incentives as inputs, and outputs a comprehensive return analysis. The model was designed to be used in a client presentation — clear, visual, and credible.

Scenario Modeled: A 100 kW commercial rooftop installation for a light manufacturing facility in the mid-Atlantic U.S. Gross system cost: $180,000. After the 30% ITC federal tax credit, net cost drops to $126,000. With 3% annual utility rate escalation, the project generates a 14.3% IRR and payback in under 7 years.

Tools Used

Microsoft Excel Python (NumPy, numpy-financial) NREL PVWatts Calculator IRS ITC Guidance (Form 3468) MACRS Depreciation Schedule EIA Utility Rate Data

Key Finance Concepts

  • Internal Rate of Return (IRR) — project-level return on invested capital
  • Net Present Value (NPV) — value creation above the required discount rate
  • Simple Payback Period vs. Discounted Payback Period
  • Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) — $/kWh over system lifetime
  • ITC (Investment Tax Credit) — 30% federal incentive on system cost
  • MACRS Accelerated Depreciation — 5-year depreciation schedule for solar
  • Utility rate escalation — compounding savings growth over 25 years

Methodology

Model Build Process

01

System Sizing & Energy Production

Used NREL's PVWatts calculator to estimate annual energy production for a 100 kW system in the mid-Atlantic region. Output: approximately 130,000 kWh per year, accounting for panel degradation of 0.5% annually over a 25-year system life.

02

Cost Model — CapEx & OpEx

CapEx: $1.80/W all-in (panels, inverters, racking, installation, permitting) = $180,000 gross. OpEx: $0.01/W/year in O&M = $1,000/year. Modeled ITC (30% of gross cost = $54,000 credit) and MACRS 5-year depreciation on 85% of net cost.

03

Revenue / Savings Model

Annual electricity savings = kWh produced × blended utility rate ($0.14/kWh = $18,200 in Year 1). Applied 3% annual utility rate escalation compounding over 25 years. Year 25 annual savings: approximately $36,900.

04

Tax Benefit Modeling

ITC: $54,000 credit applied in Year 1 against federal tax liability. MACRS depreciation: 85% of net cost ($108,900) depreciated over 5 years on an accelerated basis, generating $40,300+ in tax shield at a 28% effective rate. Total tax benefit: ~$94,300.

05

IRR, NPV & Payback Calculation

Built a 25-year cash flow model combining after-tax savings, tax benefits, and O&M costs. Calculated IRR using Excel's IRR() function; NPV using a 10% hurdle rate discount; simple and discounted payback periods. Ran sensitivity on utility escalation rate (1%–5%) and discount rate (8%–14%).

Sales Insight: Utility rate escalation is the most powerful driver of project economics. In markets where electricity rates are rising faster than 2–3% annually — increasingly common given grid modernization and carbon pricing trends — the ROI case becomes even stronger. This is the key variable to research before client presentations.

What I Learned

Key Takeaways

IRR vs. NPV in Practice

IRR is intuitive for sales conversations ("you'll earn 14% on this investment"), but NPV tells you dollar value created. Both matter depending on the audience.

Tax Policy Drives Investment

The ITC and MACRS depreciation are worth more than the Year 1 energy savings in this model. Understanding how tax incentives shape capital allocation is core finance literacy.

Finance as a Sales Tool

A well-structured ROI model closes deals. Clients trust quantified payback and IRR more than qualitative pitches — financial fluency directly enables commercial performance.

Sensitivity Is the Story

Showing clients how outcomes change under different utility rate assumptions builds credibility and addresses objections proactively. The sensitivity table is as important as the headline number.

Why It Matters for Finance

Applied Capital Budgeting

IRR and NPV are not just academic metrics — they are the primary framework through which companies, private equity firms, project finance teams, and individual investors evaluate capital expenditures. Building a working model that applies these concepts to a tangible business problem demonstrates practical proficiency, not just textbook knowledge.

For S&T roles, understanding project finance mechanics is relevant to structured products, green bonds, and infrastructure deals. For corporate finance and banking roles, capital budgeting analysis is a routine deliverable in M&A diligence and project advisory.

This project also demonstrates the commercial instinct needed for client-facing roles: translating complex financial analysis into clear, persuasive client deliverables — a skill that differentiates strong analysts across all finance disciplines.
